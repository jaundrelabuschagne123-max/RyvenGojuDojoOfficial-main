About Me & Personal Journey

Sempai Jaundre Labuschagne — 1st Dan Okinawan Goju-Ryu Karate

About Me

My name is Sempai Jaundre Labuschagne, a 1st Dan Black Belt in Okinawan Goju-Ryu Karate and a Senior Instructor within the Kokusai Kenyukan Kai Okinawa Goju-Ryu Karate/Kobudo tradition. I am also the Head Instructor of Ryven Goju Dojo.

My journey in martial arts has been built on discipline, consistency, respect, perseverance, and a commitment to continuous improvement. Karate has become more than simply a physical discipline for me. It has become a way of developing character, confidence, self-control, awareness, and the ability to remain composed under pressure.

Through years of dedicated training, I progressed through the traditional Okinawan Goju-Ryu system and eventually achieved the rank of 1st Dan. Reaching Shodan was not simply a matter of learning techniques or completing a grading. It represented years of training, correction, repetition, physical conditioning, and personal development.

As a Sempai and instructor, I believe that a martial artist should never stop being a student. Teaching others has strengthened my own understanding of Goju-Ryu by requiring me to study techniques more deeply, communicate effectively, and adapt instruction to the individual student.

My Approach to Goju-Ryu

My approach to training is centred around the traditional principles of Okinawan Goju-Ryu. Kihon, Kata, Kumite, conditioning, breathing, structure, balance, timing, distance, and practical application all have an important place in training.

I believe that technique should not simply be performed mechanically. A practitioner should understand why a technique works, how the body generates power, how movement affects balance and distance, and how traditional techniques can be applied effectively.

At the same time, I place strong importance on the traditional values of karate. Respect for instructors and fellow practitioners, humility, discipline, patience, responsibility, and perseverance are fundamental parts of martial arts training.

Teaching at Ryven Goju Dojo

As the Head Instructor of Ryven Goju Dojo, my goal is to provide a structured environment where students can develop at their own pace while maintaining the standards and traditions of Okinawan Goju-Ryu.

Every student begins their journey from a different starting point. For that reason, I believe instruction should combine traditional standards with individual attention.

Whether a student is learning their first stance, preparing for a grading, developing their Kata, improving their Kumite, or working toward advanced proficiency, the objective remains the same: continuous improvement.

Beyond the Dojo

Martial arts has also influenced the way I approach challenges outside the dojo. My background in first aid and emergency response has taught me the importance of remaining calm, assessing situations logically, and making decisions under pressure.

These qualities complement the principles I have developed through karate. Training has taught me that remaining composed, thinking clearly, and adapting to a situation are just as important as physical ability.

I believe that a martial artist should strive to be capable not only physically, but also mentally and personally. Strength should be accompanied by responsibility, knowledge by humility, and confidence by self-control.

My Personal Journey

My development in karate has been shaped by consistent training, dedication, and a willingness to learn from every stage of the journey. Each grading, training session, correction, and challenge has contributed to my development as both a martial artist and an instructor.

Achieving 1st Dan was an important milestone, but I do not regard Shodan as the end of the journey. In traditional martial arts, reaching Black Belt represents the beginning of a deeper stage of learning and responsibility.

The journey continues through training, teaching, refining technique, understanding the principles of Goju-Ryu, and passing knowledge on to the next generation of practitioners.

My Goal

My goal as an instructor is not simply to produce technically capable karate practitioners. I want to help develop disciplined and respectful martial artists who understand the value of perseverance and who carry the principles of their training beyond the dojo.

Ryven Goju Dojo is part of my continuing journey in Okinawan Goju-Ryu. Although achieving 1st Dan was an important milestone, I consider it the beginning of a new stage of learning rather than the end of the journey.

My responsibility as an instructor is to continue learning, preserve the traditions of the art, and help the next generation of students develop through disciplined and meaningful training.

Train with discipline. Move with purpose. Remain humble. Never stop learning.
